G1–G3 · HOOK / ASSEMBLY

Build density around meaning

  • First frame has a clear job
  • First claim gets matched proof
  • Premise, problem, proof, payoff stay legible
  • At least one intentional quiet or reset beat
G4A · DECODED EDITORIAL RENDER

Reject motion without a reason

  • Unearned full-screen takeovers
  • Evidence collisions or proof arriving late
  • Unmotivated punch-ins and repeated same-mode changes
  • Long claim-bearing gaps and redundant simultaneous text
G4B–G5 · LOCKED MASTER

Verify the pixels and the reset

  • Rhythm/timing edits create a new lock version
  • Accessibility captions stay separate from semantic text
  • Semantic role: headline, impact, proof label, or CTA
  • Inspect decoded pixels, final audio, and delivery size

The opening was denser than the body. Quiet A-roll is not a failure when it lets emotion land, restores attention, or creates contrast. Fast change is useful only when each change advances the case.

BRANCH / VERTICAL-REACTION

Vertical reaction

Speech-led, source-bounded
source context → response selects → transcript-led A-cut → fine cut
WHY THIS ROUTE

Speech usually owns the argument; the reacted-to source supplies the minimum truthful context.

LOCK EMPHASIS

Source attribution, first seconds, presenter/source geometry, title, captions, and mobile safe zones.

COMMON FAILURE

Treating a style precedent as the content source or overbuilding finish before the response cut lands.

VLOG RULE dailies → stringout → selects → story assembly → rough cut

A transcript sees dialogue, not silent action, chronology, location, visual motifs, reaction shots, or missing coverage. It can propose interview ranges; it cannot fully choose the vlog story.

Approval vocabulary

Stop calling everything “final.”

Every word below closes a different risk. Name the state that actually passed.

ASSEMBLYStory exists

Complete order; loose timing and temporary finish.

ROUGH CUTDirection review

Structure can still change materially.

FINE CUTEditorial candidate

Shot choice and timing are precise enough to approve.

EDITORIAL-APPROVEDEdit accepted

Story and picture timing, not final craft.

PICTURE-LOCKEDClock frozen

Turnovers may rely on one exact timeline.

MASTER-APPROVEDFinish accepted

Picture, mix, color, graphics, captions, and corrections.

QC-PASSEDSpec verified

Exact deliverable survives technical and perceptual checks.

DELIVERED / ARCHIVEDHanded off / recoverable

Destination receipt and restore evidence exist.

Right-sized professional practice

Borrow the risk controls. Skip the theater.

BORROW FROM STUDIOS

Practices that prevent expensive mistakes

  • Checksum-verified offload before erasing a card
  • Stable metadata, sync, proxies, and a test conform
  • Dailies/selects before story commitment
  • Rough, fine, editorial-approved, picture-locked, master-approved states
  • Locked reference and versioned turnovers
  • Timecoded notes with owner and disposition
  • Separate picture, sound, color, text, and delivery checks
  • Delivery QC and a recoverable archive receipt
SKIP THE BUREAUCRACY

Practices that only simulate team size

  • Department meetings when one person owns every role
  • Approval committees for reversible low-risk shorts
  • Broadcast paperwork for a simple web deliverable
  • Proxies when originals already play smoothly
  • Interchange turnovers when one timeline safely owns the finish
  • Duplicate ledgers and ceremonial status reports
  • Formal vendor packages for every temp cue or style frame
  • Any field that does not prevent loss, rework, or ambiguity
